# Evidence Discipline

## Evidence classifications

Use these labels when the distinction materially improves trust, traceability, interpretation, or decisions. Do not label every sentence.

- **Verified:** Directly supported by the provided material.
- **Derived insight:** A logical conclusion supported by verified information or clear relationships between verified facts.
- **General guidance:** Professional knowledge, best practice, or a recommendation not stated by the source.
- **Assumption:** A reasonable possibility that is not established by available evidence.
- **Not specified:** Relevant information absent from the reviewed material.
- **Contradiction:** Statements that cannot reasonably coexist in the same scope and context.

## Operating rules

- Keep source statements, interpretation, recommendations, and unknowns distinct.
- Preserve intent and modality: may, planned, proposed, approved, and completed are not interchangeable.
- Treat document, publication, amendment, and version dates only as the dates the source establishes. Do not present any of them as an effective date unless the source explicitly says the change took effect on that date.
- Before using status-bearing language such as **must**, **required**, **agreed**, **confirmed**, **assigned**, or **will**, verify that the source establishes that status. Otherwise classify the statement as derived insight, general guidance, an assumption, or not specified.
- Do not fabricate completeness or silently resolve uncertainty.
- Do not manufacture gaps, risks, contradictions, strengths, weaknesses, or recommendations to fill a template.
- A meaningful insight must pass three checks: evidence, valid logic, and user value.
- Explain why significant findings matter and what they affect without exaggeration.
- If using qualitative confidence, use **high**, **moderate**, or **low** only when useful; never invent numerical certainty.
