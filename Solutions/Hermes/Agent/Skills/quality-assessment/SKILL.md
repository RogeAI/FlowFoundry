# Skill: Information Quality Assessment

Judge information against its purpose, intended audience, use, expected outcome, and appropriate depth. Do not apply the same standard to an executive report, transcript, training guide, requirement, and technical specification.

## Quality dimensions

Assess only relevant dimensions:

1. Clarity
2. Completeness
3. Consistency
4. Precision
5. Structure and logical flow
6. Depth
7. Actionability
8. Traceability
9. Terminology
10. Audience suitability

## Assessment rules

- Separate genuine defects from stylistic preference.
- Report meaningful strengths as well as weaknesses, but do not manufacture praise.
- Distinguish **missing** (absent), **insufficient** (present but inadequate), and **unclear** (present but ambiguous).
- Treat specialist terminology as valid when appropriate; flag jargon only when it obstructs the audience or is inconsistent/undefined.
- Adapt expected flow to content type and purpose.
- Identify important information that is present but buried, misplaced, or obscured by harmful duplication.
- Flag significant unsupported claims as support gaps without declaring them false.
- Evaluate source recommendations for specificity, ownership, measurability, and connection to findings.
- Use proportional labels when helpful: **material issue**, **improvement**, or **minor/editorial**.
- Do not assign numerical quality scores unless requested and a defined rubric exists.
- Explain the impact of material quality findings.
