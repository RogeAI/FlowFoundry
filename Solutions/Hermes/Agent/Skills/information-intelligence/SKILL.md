# Skill: Information Intelligence

## Purpose

Turn one or more sources into useful understanding rather than a shorter pile of words.

Use this skill for broad synthesis when the primary objective is to understand what matters across supplied material. Use Source Analysis for explicit source or version comparison, Quality Assessment for fitness-for-purpose review, and Recommendations and Actions when decisions or actions are the primary output.

## Capabilities

- Summarize and synthesize while preserving important context and disagreement.
- Extract significant facts, requirements, dependencies, changes, themes, and unresolved matters.
- Generate meaningful insights by connecting evidence and explaining implications.
- Distinguish facts, intent, proposals, opinions, decisions, commitments, and actions.
- Identify material gaps, ambiguities, unsupported claims, inconsistencies, contradictions, risks, and questions.
- Produce management or executive synthesis focused on state, change, impact, decisions, dependencies, blockers, and attention required.

## Workflow

1. Understand what was provided and the user's objective.
2. Select only relevant analytical capabilities.
3. Establish verified evidence and preserve context.
4. Separate signal from repetition, filler, and unrelated detail.
5. Connect dependencies, sequences, patterns, cause and effect, changes, and cross-source relationships.
6. Classify problems accurately; do not call every absence a gap or every difference a contradiction.
7. Derive insights using evidence, logic, and value.
8. Explain proportionate significance.
9. Continue around non-blocking missing information and expose important unknowns.
10. Build an importance-first response and run the final quality gate.

## Adaptive output

- **Focused:** direct answer, key insight, and any material issue.
- **Standard:** optional TL;DR, key information, insights, material problems, decisions/actions, and recommendations.
- **Full:** executive summary plus relevant scope, findings, relationships, decisions, commitments, actions, risks, gaps, contradictions, questions, and recommendations.

These are choices, not mandatory templates. Preserve nuance, omit empty sections, and follow the user's requested format.
