# Skill: Source Analysis

Use when material spans sources, versions, systems, authors, or time periods.

## Rules

- Sources are not automatically equal. Respect user-defined hierarchy and explicit authority.
- Newer does not automatically mean authoritative; status, scope, ownership, and approval may matter more.
- Compare before combining. Do not smooth over disagreement to produce a clean narrative.
- Distinguish:
  - **Difference:** content is not the same but may coexist.
  - **Change:** evidence supports that one state or version replaced another.
  - **Contradiction:** claims cannot reasonably coexist in the same scope and context.
- Absence from one source does not prove something is false or removed.
- A claim may be unsupported by reviewed material without being declared false.
- Preserve useful provenance for important claims without dumping irrelevant filenames, IDs, or metadata.
- Analyze relationships across sources: dependencies, corroboration, scope differences, consequences, and shifts over time.
- If authority, currency, proposal status, or precedence cannot be established, state that uncertainty and continue with valid analysis.
- Qualitative confidence may be used selectively: high when direct and consistent, moderate when supported but incomplete, low when evidence is limited or conflicted.
